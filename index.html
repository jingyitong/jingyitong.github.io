Current Projects

  • - Experimental design and information treatment analysis for farmers’ willingness to adopt conservation practices, with a focus on measuring willingness-to-pay (WTP), reducing hypothetical bias, and evaluating how different information presentations affect adoption intentions.
  • - Field experiment on consumer willingness-to-pay (WTP) for cucumbers grown under different production systems, with or without new technology of row covers, examining how pesticide and plastic use affect consumer preferences.
  • - Impacts of different policies, such as crop insurance and the Conservation Reserve Program, and technologies, such as genetically modified seeds, on land use, crop yields, and fertilizer application, and their subsequent effects on environmental pollution.
  • - Consumer willingness to pay for new energy materials, such as plastic products made from green ethylene.
  • - Health impacts of PFAS contamination.

Extension & Outreach

Women Landowners’ Voices in Iowa

Women own nearly half of all acres in Iowa and are an essential group in agriculture and natural resources. This project develops tailored educational materials to improve the knowledge and confidence of women landowners on farmland leasing, soil and water conservation practices, and efficient farmland transition planning.
Award: 2023 AAEA Graduate Student Extension Competition — Second Place.
Publications: Tong et al. (2024), Renewable Agriculture and Food Systems; Schultz et al. (2022), Journal of The NACAA; Tong & Zhang (2022), CARD Policy Brief.
Presentations: Extension Risk Management National Conference (Apr. 2024); Women in Ag Leadership Conference (Nov. 2022).

Iowa Farmland Ownership and Tenure Survey

Using a statistically representative sample of Iowa landowners (2022), this project provides a 40-year perspective (1982–present) on land ownership, tenure, acquisition, succession, and farmland characteristics. The 2022 survey also examines working land and edge-of-field conservation practices, trust usage, and potential transfers to beginning farmers.
Publications: Tong & Zhang (2023), Iowa State University Extension and Outreach, FM 1893; Hart, Tong & Zhang (2023), Agricultural Policy Review.
Presentation: Iowa Nutrient Research Center (Oct. 2023).

What Drives Organic Cucurbit Growers to Adopt Mesotunnel Row Covers?

This study assesses the adoption potential of mesotunnels — medium-sized, breathable nylon-mesh structures — among organic cucurbit growers. Applying the Technology Acceptance Model 3 (TAM3) and ordered logit models on 337 survey responses, results show a marginal increase in perceived efficacy or ease of use increases the likelihood of adoption by 1.55× and 2.34×, respectively.
Presentation: AFHVS/ASFS Conference, June 2024.
