CV (English) 中文简历

I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate at the Charles H. Dyson School of Applied Economics and Management at Cornell University. I received my Ph.D. in Agricultural Economics from Iowa State University in 2025.

My research is in agricultural and environmental economics and experimental economics. I study how farmers, landowners, and consumers make decisions about conservation, land use, and new agricultural and energy technologies, and how policy and information shape those decisions. My work combines field and survey experiments, discrete choice experiments, and satellite-based land use data.

Research interests: conservation practice adoption, farmland tenure and ownership, cropland use and agricultural pollution, technology adoption, consumer willingness to pay, experiment design, and women in agriculture.

Recent Projects I am Working on

  • Farmers’ willingness to adopt conservation practices. Experimental design and information treatments to measure farmers’ willingness to pay for conservation practices, reduce hypothetical bias, and test how the presentation of information shapes adoption intentions.
  • Consumer demand for sustainably grown produce. A field experiment on consumer willingness to pay for cucumbers grown under different production systems, with and without row cover technology, examining how pesticide and plastic use affect consumer preferences.
  • Farm policy, land use, and pollution. How policies such as crop insurance and the Conservation Reserve Program, and technologies such as genetically modified seeds, affect land use, crop yields, and fertilizer application, and the resulting environmental pollution.
  • Demand for new energy technologies and materials. Consumer willingness to pay for mobile microgrids and for plastic products made from green ethylene.
  • Agrivoltaics in New York State. Cost and benefit analysis of combining solar energy and agriculture on commercial farms.
  • PFAS and public health. Associations between PFAS contamination and chronic disease and cancer across U.S. counties.
